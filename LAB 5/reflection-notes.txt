Question:
While your order's setTimeout is “waiting,” is the Node.js process frozen?
Answer:
While setTimeout is waiting, the Node.js process is not completely frozen.
The timer is handled outside the main JavaScript execution flow and Node.js can continue handling other work.
When the timer finishes, its callback becomes ready to be executed by the Event Loop.
The Event Loop checks when the call stack is free and then executes the callback.
This is why Node.js can handle asynchronous operations without blocking the whole program.

Question:
Why does Promise.all() finish faster?
Answer:
Promise.all() starts all the asynchronous operations at approximately the same time.
They do not wait for one order to finish before starting the next order.
Therefore, the total time is usually close to the longest individual operation.
If I used three separate await statements one after another, each operation would start only after the previous one finished.
So sequential await would take approximately the sum of all three delays.

Question: 
Does Promise.all() take the SUM of all three delays, or roughly the LONGEST single delay? Explain why.
Answer:
Promise.all() takes roughly the longest single delay, not the sum of all three delays. This is because all three orders start at the same time and run concurrently. Promise.all() waits until all the promises are completed, so the total time is approximately equal to the slowest order. In my test, all three orders completed in about 3.255 seconds.
